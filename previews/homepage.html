Association of Chief AI Officers Australia

AI is becoming an enterprise capability.
AI leadership must become a professional discipline.

ACAIO is being established as an independent, not-for-profit professional institution for executives accountable for enterprise AI in Australian organisations.

For Chief AI Officers and equivalent leaders with enterprise-wide AI responsibility, whatever their title.

Enterprise accountability needs a professional foundation.

Enterprise AI brings connected decisions about investment, operating models, technology, risk and people. Executives need to bring those decisions together and take responsibility for the outcomes.

ACAIO is being established to help develop the shared standards, competencies and practices that this accountability requires.

One discipline. Six connected domains.

Enterprise AI leadership integrates these disciplines to convert rapidly evolving AI capability into sustainable organisational performance.

Strategy

Enterprise AI strategy, portfolio choices, investment and alignment.

Transformation

Operating-model and process transformation enabled by AI.

Value

Business cases, benefits, productivity, growth and value realisation.

Capability

Data, platforms, models, agents, architecture, vendors and organisational capability.

Governance

Responsible AI, risk, assurance, accountability, regulation and board oversight.

Adoption

Workforce, skills, change, behaviours and organisational adoption.

A professional institution for enterprise AI leadership.

ACAIO's purpose is to professionalise the leadership of artificial intelligence in Australian organisations. Its proposed work has four priorities.

Define the profession

Develop shared accountabilities, competencies and professional standards for enterprise AI leadership.

Build the executive community

Bring together practitioners across sectors to exchange experience and help shape the discipline.

Develop practical knowledge

Build a Body of Knowledge, frameworks and research grounded in enterprise practice.

Develop a representative voice

Create a foundation for enterprise AI leaders to contribute informed perspectives to policy and professional debate.

About ACAIO

Help define an emerging profession.

ACAIO is bringing together a Founding Council of senior practitioners to help shape enterprise AI leadership in Australia.

The proposed Council will help define the profession's accountabilities, competencies, Body of Knowledge and research priorities. Its work will inform the institution as it develops.

We welcome expressions of interest from executives with enterprise AI accountability across Australian business, government and higher education.

The Founding Council is distinct from the legal Board. An expression of interest does not confer Council membership or a director appointment.

Start with the foundations of professional practice.

The proposed initial agenda focuses on five outputs. Their scope and priorities will be shaped with founding participants.

Professional Charter

A shared statement of the profession's purpose, principles and responsibilities.

CAIO Competency Framework

A framework for the capabilities required to lead enterprise AI.

Leadership & Accountability Framework

Clarity on leadership responsibilities, governance and accountability for enterprise AI.

Enterprise AI Maturity Model

A basis for assessing organisational progress and identifying priorities for development.

State of Enterprise AI Leadership in Australia

A proposed research report examining how the discipline is developing in Australian organisations.

Professional credibility starts with independence.

ACAIO is being developed around six founding principles. The intended model keeps professional standards and research independent of vendor and sponsor control.

Practitioner-led

Shaped by executives accountable for enterprise AI.

Independent

Vendors and sponsors do not control standards or research.

Evidence-based

Grounded in enterprise practice and credible research.

Responsible

Focused on value creation while keeping AI governable.

Collaborative

Complements rather than attempts to own the entire Australian AI ecosystem.

Profession-first

Institutional credibility takes priority over rapid monetisation.
